3.4 NARRATIVE AND THEMATIC SYNTHESIS
================================================

THIS SECTION MUST BE SHORT AND JOURNAL-LIKE.

The current implementation produces overly long Section 3.4 content.

Do NOT generate a long AI report.

Section 3.4 should normally contain:

3.4 Narrative and Thematic Synthesis

A short introductory paragraph.

Then approximately 3–5 thematic subsections ONLY IF supported by the included evidence.

Example:

3.4.1 System-Level Decarbonisation

Concise integrated synthesis.

3.4.2 Alternative Fuel Pathways

Concise integrated synthesis.

3.4.3 Technological and Operational Strategies

Concise integrated synthesis.

Do NOT force 3–5 themes if the evidence supports fewer themes.

If only 2 strong themes exist, use 2.

If one dominant theme exists, use one.

Do NOT manufacture additional themes merely to make the section look comprehensive.

------------------------------------------------
3.4 LENGTH LIMIT
------------------------------------------------

Target total length:

approximately 600–1,000 words maximum.

Prefer the shorter end when the evidence is limited.

Each thematic subsection should normally be:

approximately 150–250 words maximum.

The introductory paragraph should be approximately 50–100 words.

Do not exceed these limits unless the evidence genuinely requires additional explanation.

Do NOT repeat the same information across themes.

Do NOT repeat the Results section.

Do NOT repeat the Discussion section.

Do NOT provide an exhaustive description of every study.

The purpose of Section 3.4 is to synthesize the major patterns across the included studies.

------------------------------------------------
3.4 THEMATIC HEADINGS
------------------------------------------------

The current heading:

“Decarbonisation is framed as a system-level transition in fuel supply, fleets, and infrastructure”

is TOO LONG and NOT suitable as a manuscript subsection heading.

Headings must normally be 2–6 words.

They must be concise noun phrases.

GOOD:
- System-Level Decarbonisation
- Alternative Fuel Pathways
- Technological Innovation
- Fleet and Infrastructure Transition
- Operational Decarbonisation

BAD:
- Decarbonisation is framed as a system-level transition in fuel supply, fleets, and infrastructure
- The literature demonstrates the importance of alternative fuels for reducing emissions
- Studies show that technological innovation can improve environmental performance

The heading identifies the theme.

The prose explains the evidence.

Do not use sentence-like headings.

Do not use mini-abstracts as headings.

------------------------------------------------
3.4 THEME DISCOVERY
------------------------------------------------

Themes must be derived from the actual included evidence.

Do NOT use a hard-coded keyword classifier.

Remove or stop using legacy mechanisms such as:
- outcomeTerms
- technologyTerms
- methodsTerms
- classifyTheme
- classifyRecordTheme
- buildClusters
- themeDefinitions
- hard-coded maritime categories
- title substring matching for thematic classification

Do not classify studies simply because their titles contain certain keywords.

Themes should reflect recurring concepts, approaches, interventions, outcomes, contexts, or relationships actually present across the included studies.

If evidence does not support a thematic distinction, do not create one.

------------------------------------------------
3.4 NARRATIVE STYLE
------------------------------------------------

The synthesis must be an integrated narrative.

DO NOT write an annotated bibliography.

Avoid:

“Author A found...”
“Author B reported...”
“Author C examined...”
“Author D showed...”

Do not write one paragraph per study.

Instead:

1. Introduce the broad pattern.
2. Integrate evidence from multiple studies.
3. Compare or contrast studies where appropriate.
4. Interpret the pattern.
5. Identify a gap only when the included evidence supports it.

Use transitions between studies and concepts.

The section should read like a scholarly synthesis rather than a list of papers.

Do not force every study to appear explicitly in the prose.

Do not fabricate record IDs.

The application should format citations after the AI response according to the user's selected citation style.

5. 3.2 CHARACTERISTICS OF INCLUDED STUDIES
================================================

Keep this section concise.

Do NOT display publication-year distribution.

Do NOT display:

“Not reported”

as a category.

Do NOT display:

“Not established from citation metadata”

If a characteristic has no meaningful reported values across the included studies, OMIT that characteristic completely.

For example, do NOT show:

Context | Not reported | 11

Instead, only display characteristics for which actual information exists.

Example:

| Characteristic | Category | n |
|---|---|---:|
| Study/intervention category | Energy, emissions and environmental performance | 11 |

Use actual StudyCharacteristic data.

Do NOT infer characteristics from:
- title
- keywords
- journal
- authors
- publication year

unless the information has actually been extracted into the corresponding StudyCharacteristic field.

The unit of analysis is ONE INCLUDED STUDY.

Never count:

- JSON fields
- tokens
- keyword occurrences

For 11 included studies, a category count can never be 161.

Every displayed count must represent the number of included records.

- sample sizes
- datasets
- effect sizes
- confidence intervals
- p-values
- I²
- statistical significance
- experimental outcomes
- model performance

if they are not available.

Maintain distinctions between:
- measured outcomes;
- calculated/estimated outcomes;
- proxy indicators;
- model-predicted outcomes;
- simulation outcomes;
- intended or claimed reductions.

Model accuracy does not equal demonstrated decarbonisation.

Simulation results do not automatically equal real-world outcomes.
